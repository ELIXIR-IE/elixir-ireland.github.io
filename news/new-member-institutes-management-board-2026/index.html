ELIXIR Ireland Welcomes Five New Member Institutes

DCU, MTU, SETU, TCD and TU Dublin join the Node, each with a new representative on the ELIXIR Ireland Management Board.

25 October 2026

ELIXIR Ireland has doubled to ten member institutes. Dublin City University (DCU), Munster Technological University (MTU), South East Technological University (SETU), Trinity College Dublin (TCD) and Technological University Dublin (TU Dublin) have joined the Node. Each now has a representative on the ELIXIR Ireland Management Board, where every member institute holds a vote on the Node's key decisions.

We welcomed our new members at the ELIXIR Ireland onboarding session on 22 October 2026, an introduction to VIBE, ELIXIR Europe and ELIXIR Ireland presented by Aedín Culhane, Head of Node, and Gavin Farrell, Node Coordinator, at the University of Limerick (UL). The full recording is below.

Meet our new Management Board members

Daniel Murphy, DCU

Dr Daniel Murphy is Assistant Professor in Computational Biology in the School of Biotechnology at DCU. His research interests are in genomics, transcriptomics, clinical bioinformatics and precision medicine. He previously worked at the Wellcome Sanger Institute and EMBL-EBI helping to define reference gene sets, including the Ensembl gene annotation system and the MANE transcript set for clinical genomics. He then worked as a clinical bioinformatician at the Mater Misericordiae University Hospital, in genomics industry, and as a lecturer in bioinformatics at University College Dublin (UCD).

Daniel is also hosting VIBE 2026 at DCU Glasnevin on 3 December.

Francesca Bottacini, MTU

Dr Francesca Bottacini is a Lecturer in the Department of Biological Sciences at MTU, with a joint affiliation at APC Microbiome Ireland, University College Cork (UCC). Her research applies comparative genomics to beneficial gut bacteria, particularly bifidobacteria: their genome diversity, how they colonise and interact with the infant gut, and their interactions with bacteriophages. Recent work includes genome-scale resources for the infant gut symbiont Bifidobacterium breve (Cell, 2025) and studies of phage defence systems in lactococci (Nucleic Acids Research, 2025).

John Ronan, SETU

John Ronan is Infrastructure Group Manager at the Walton Institute, SETU, where he has been technical lead of the Infrastructure Group since 2016. His work covers research computing infrastructure, OpenStack cloud and networking, with research interests in networks, security and the Internet of Things. John has already worked with ELIXIR Ireland through EOSC Ireland, and brings infrastructure expertise that complements the Node's life science focus.

Máire Ní Leathlobhair, TCD

Dr Máire Ní Leathlobhair is an Assistant Professor in the School of Genetics and Microbiology at TCD. Her research brings genomics and evolutionary biology to cancer, including rare and clonally transmissible cancers. Recent work includes the genomic landscape of adult testicular germ cell tumours in the 100,000 Genomes Project (Nature Communications, 2024) and chromosomal instability in hypodiploid tumours (Genome Medicine, 2026).

Máire hosted VIBE 2025 at TCD and was local host of the ELIXIR Federated Human Data Community meeting in Dublin this October.

TCD profile · ORCID

Therese Murphy, TU Dublin

Dr Therese Murphy is a Lecturer in the School of Biological, Health and Sports Sciences at TU Dublin. Her research focuses on the epigenomics of complex diseases, particularly depression and inflammatory disorders, combining DNA methylation profiling with machine learning for biomarker discovery. Recent work includes a methylome-wide association study of major depression (Nature Mental Health, 2025) and blood DNA methylation signatures of ageing and disease progression in inflammatory bowel disease (Human Genetics, 2025).
