EOSC Ireland Service Provider Readiness Survey

EOSC Ireland is mapping the national research service landscape. If you run an operational research service in Ireland, in any research domain, we would like to hear about it.

The EOSC Ireland project is designing Ireland's national node of the European Open Science Cloud, and it needs an accurate picture of the research services this country actually runs. The Service Provider Readiness Survey is now open to every operational research service hosted or managed in Ireland: data repositories, curated databases, compute clusters, storage, analysis platforms, workflow and interoperability services, and training portals.

What EOSC Ireland is, and what it is not

EOSC Ireland will serve as the national connection point to the wider EOSC Federation, a network of national and thematic EOSC Nodes. EOSC Ireland will not host or manage your service.

A useful way to picture it: EOSC Ireland is a national shopping centre for research services. Service providers operate their own individual shops, retaining total control over their own infrastructure, resource allocation and access policies, while gaining access to a wider user base.

Two questions come up more than any others, so they are worth answering up front:

  • There is no financial cost charged by EOSC Ireland to onboard a service. Deeper technical integration would take effort, and sizing that effort is exactly what this survey is for. Providers keep their own access policies, including quota or wallet models where those apply.
  • "Service" is broader than you might think. If researchers depend on it operationally, it very likely belongs in this survey, even if you have never thought of it as an "EOSC" thing.

Who should respond

Operational national or domain-specific research services hosted or managed in Ireland. If you host more than one very distinct, non-overlapping service, a separate response for each is ideal.

In scope

  • Data repositories and archives
  • Curated knowledgebases and thematic databases
  • General-purpose storage and data transfer
  • Compute, including HPC clusters
  • Scientific software, tools and analysis platforms
  • Interoperability and standards services
  • Training and e-learning portals

Out of scope

  • Off-the-shelf commercial tools
  • Small-scale individual research scripts
  • Personal project websites
  • Raw, unmanaged datasets
  • Physical scientific instrumentation

Every research domain, not just the life sciences

ELIXIR Ireland is a life science research infrastructure, and this survey reaches well beyond that. The national map should look like Irish research actually looks: physics and astronomy, engineering, chemistry and materials, environment and marine, agriculture and food, energy, social sciences, humanities and cultural heritage, plus the HPC, storage and research IT teams holding it all up.

If you run something operational that researchers depend on, it belongs in this survey. Not sure whether your service is in scope? It very likely is, or email gavin.farrell@ul.ie and ask.

What the survey covers

The survey is organised into ten short sections. Most are multiple choice; a handful ask for a short paragraph.

1. About the responder

Your role, primary institution, and any secondary affiliations such as national institutes or clinical academic posts.

2. About the service

Name, public URL, primary research domain (aligned with ESFRI divisions), service type, what it does, who uses it, and how large the user base is.

3. EOSC awareness & wider audience

Your current awareness of EOSC and its technical standards, any national mandate on the service, and whether you have considered opening it to a wider national or European audience.

4. Staffing & sustainability

Staff allocation in FTE, the operational management model, funding sources, and whether the service is sustained infrastructure or a time-bound project.

5. Availability & technical setup

How users interact with the service: web portal, machine API, CLI, HPC queuing system, SaaS/DaaS, downloadable package, source repository, and so on.

6. Authentication & authorisation (AAI)

Whether login is required and which frameworks are supported (eduGAIN, EOSC AAI, Life Science AAI, ORCID, OIDC, API keys), plus any vetting workflows for sensitive data.

7. FAIR service metadata

Registration in discovery registries such as FAIRsharing, re3data or bio.tools; metadata standards in use; and the data access protocols the service exposes.

8. Needs for onboarding & support

Your onboarding intent, and a ranking of the supports you would need: staffing capacity, integration funding, technical consultancy, security and governance advice, or training.

9. Workshop & data protection

Opt in to the 19 October workshop, and review the research privacy notice under which responses are processed.

10. Additional services

A place to list other services you manage but cannot complete a full response for, keeping your wider portfolio on the EOSC Ireland radar.

Key terms used in the survey

Service onboarding: making a service discoverable on the EOSC Ireland Node's service catalogue. The process is likely to be phased: first a minimal set of standardised metadata, and only later any deeper technical integration.

Service federation: making a service interoperable across EOSC Nodes through common standards, protocols and delivery practices, described in section 4 of the EOSC Federation Handbook.

Service Provider Readiness Workshop

Monday 19 October 2026, 10:00–12:00 (Irish Standard Time) · Online via Microsoft Teams · Free to attend

The joining link and calendar invite are sent directly to registered participants closer to the date.

Who this is for

Anyone who operates a research service in Ireland, national or domain-specific, including data repositories and archives, curated databases, compute and storage services, analysis platforms, workflow and interoperability services, and training or e-learning portals. You do not need to have completed the survey to attend, and you do not need any prior knowledge of EOSC.

Purpose

The workshop introduces the EOSC Ireland project alongside the service onboarding and federation concepts that matter to Irish research service providers. Most of the session is given over to structured discussion in breakout groups, to understand and evaluate the current readiness, diversity and structure of national services, and to identify the technical and integration supports providers would need to onboard to the future EOSC Ireland platform.

Put plainly: we are trying to work out what would have to be true before any provider is asked to integrate anything. The people who can answer that are the people in the room.

Agenda

Time Session
10:00 – 10:05 Sign-in and welcome: orientation, and links to the agenda and slides
10:05 – 10:25 EOSC Ireland in context: what EOSC and the EOSC Federation are; the project, partners and timeline; the planned services and platform; what onboarding and federation involve in practice; what the provider survey told us; the path forward (recorded)
10:25 – 10:30 Clarifications: questions on the presentation, shared document links, and how the discussion will run
10:30 – 10:35 Framing the breakouts: the questions each group will work through, and how groups are formed (by service type, six to eight per group)
10:35 – 11:00 Breakout discussions: by service type: compute, data, tools, training, standards and metadata. What are the benefits of federating a service? What are the obstacles, concerns and drawbacks? What supports would you need in order to onboard?
11:00 – 11:10 Break
11:10 – 11:30 Breakouts continue: groups agree their three main takeaways
11:30 – 12:00 Reporting back and open discussion: three takeaways per group (two to three minutes each), then open discussion of provider needs and next steps

Only the 10:05–10:25 presentation is recorded. Breakout discussions are not recorded; notes are taken and reported back in aggregate.

What to bring

Nothing to prepare. It helps if you can speak to one service you run: roughly who uses it, how it is funded and staffed, and how users get access, though the breakouts are a discussion, not a form.

ELIXIR Ireland's role

ELIXIR Ireland leads WP4: Connecting to the EOSC Federation within the EOSC Ireland project, through the University of Limerick. Gavin Farrell leads the work package as part of the Culhane lab group at UL, and runs this survey. WP4 is responsible for identifying the technical and policy requirements for connecting Irish research assets to the EOSC Federation, with a focus on interoperability, metadata standards and use case development, which is precisely what the survey and workshop are gathering the evidence for.

For the wider background on how ELIXIR Ireland is involved, see ELIXIR Ireland contributes to EOSC Ireland and ELIXIR Ireland joins EOSC Gravity grant to write Ireland's EOSC Node Charter.
