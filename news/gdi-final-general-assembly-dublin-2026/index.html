Final GDI General Assembly held in Dublin

The European Genomic Data Infrastructure project brought its partners to Dublin for its final General Assembly, reflecting on the past year and planning the closing months of the project.

12 October 2026

The European Genomic Data Infrastructure (GDI) project held its final General Assembly in Dublin in early October, in person and online, during Ireland's Presidency of the Council of the European Union. GDI is the backbone of the 1+ Million Genomes (1+MG) initiative, building a federated, cross-border network of national genome collections for research and healthcare. The General Assembly was organised by the GDI project.

National deployment and connectivity demonstrated

Partners reflected on the achievements of the past year and discussed plans for the remainder of the project. The 1+MG infrastructure deployment pillar demonstrated national deployment and connectivity, using examples of Genome of Europe data, cancer use case data and synthetic data. Each GDI node country then presented its progress towards connecting to the GDI user portal and its aims to March 2027.

With five months remaining, the project is focused on delivering its final outcomes and advancing the European Genomic Data Infrastructure.

Ireland in GDI

Ireland takes part through GDI Ireland, the Irish arm of the project, co-led by RCSI and University College Dublin (UCD) in partnership with the University of Limerick (UL) and Maynooth University. Its core management team overlaps closely with ELIXIR Ireland:

RCSI (co-lead)

  • Gianpiero Cavalleri: GDI Ireland co-lead, coordinating long-term sustainability and the design of a public genome sequencing project. RCSI representative on the ELIXIR Ireland Management Board.
  • Laura Whelan: sustainability and genome sequencing project work. ELIXIR Ireland Deputy Head of Node.
  • Creina Slator: programme manager across all project activities.

University College Dublin (UCD) (co-lead)

  • Denis Shields: GDI Ireland co-lead, coordinating education and training. UCD representative on the ELIXIR Ireland Management Board.

University of Limerick (UL)

  • Aedín Culhane: leads the technical implementation. ELIXIR Ireland Head of Node.
  • Alex Mahmoud: research software engineer, technical implementation.
  • Abhishek Nayak: PhD researcher, technical implementation.

Maynooth University

  • Markus Helfert: leads the work on data governance.
  • Denise Manton: data governance.

Together they connect national work on sensitive genomic data with ELIXIR Ireland's wider role in federated human data.
